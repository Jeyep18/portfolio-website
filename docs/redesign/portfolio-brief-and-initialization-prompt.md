# John Francis Espiritu: portfolio redesign brief and initialization prompt

Prepared November 10, 2026. This is a proposed design direction and a planning prompt, not approval to implement or deploy.

## Proposed direction

Working concept: a quiet observatory for ideas and the work behind them. This is an internal design metaphor, not a required public title or navigation theme.

John's preferences are clean, predominantly monochrome compositions, space, NASA, science and physics, and the sense of scale and atmosphere he associates with Interstellar. Brittany Chiang is the strongest portfolio reference. The site should combine her clarity with optional project inspection and selective editorial notes. John likes the idea of personality in Josh Comeau's site, but not its visual direction.

The desired impression is: John notices meaningful problems, develops considered ideas, and takes care in bringing them to life. The portfolio should support freelance inquiries, creative frontend opportunities, software engineering internships, and a lasting personal presence.

Recommended visual treatment:

- Near-black or charcoal backgrounds, off-white text, restrained gray divisions, and one muted accent chosen through previews.
- A single original atmospheric composition: an illuminated horizon, an eclipse-like edge, or a restrained orbital study. Explore these as alternatives, not three simultaneous effects.
- Space expressed through negative space, scale, light, and fine annotation rather than a crowded star field or literal spaceship interface.
- Clear sans-serif text; small monospaced metadata only where useful. Typography, contrast, and sizing must remain readable.
- Asymmetric but orderly editorial layouts. Use real project imagery prominently and keep the atmosphere visually secondary to the work.
- Direct navigation, with name, work, resume, and contact easy to find. Optional discovery must not become a prerequisite for reading.
- A small, meaningful project-inspection interaction, based on actual project material, rather than decorative complexity.
